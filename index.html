The SETI post-detection protocols and Committee records

The IAA SETI Committee is the international forum for the scientific, technical and societal aspects of the search for extraterrestrial intelligence. It organises the IAA SETI Symposium at the International Astronautical Congress (IAC) each year. Its most important responsibility is the SETI post-detection protocols: the principles scientists should follow if they detect evidence of extraterrestrial intelligence. This site sets out the current protocols and every earlier version since 1989, together with the records of the Committee's meetings.

Current

Declaration of Principles Concerning the Conduct of the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence (SETI) – 2026 Update

The current protocols, rewritten for a search that now spans many kinds of technosignature and for a media environment shaped by social media and AI. Its eight principles cover handling candidate evidence, communicating during verification, reporting a confirmed detection, archiving and data access, data and frequency protection, a standing Post-Detection Sub-Committee, replying to a detection, and ethical and legal considerations. New provisions include steps to protect the safety of researchers, a right for individuals to decline media engagement, prompt disclosure when a candidate is ruled out, peer-reviewed verification reports, and archiving in at least two geographically separate repositories.

Published by the International Academy of Astronautics · 1 June 2026

Prepared by the IAA SETI Committee Post-Detection Task Group, chaired by Michael Garrett, with Kathryn Denning, Les Tennen and Carol Oliver.

30 September 2010Declaration of Principles Concerning the Conduct of the Search for Extraterrestrial IntelligenceSuperseded

A streamlined replacement for the 1989 Declaration, in eight principles. It adds a commitment to transparent searching and responsiveness to the media, and says there is no obligation to disclose verification work while it is under way. The Rio Scale is recommended for conveying the significance of candidate signals. Confirmed detections go to the public, the scientific community, the UN Secretary-General and the IAU. The Declaration keeps data archiving and ITU frequency protection, refers to a Post-Detection Task Group, and says signatories will not respond to a confirmed signal without first seeking the guidance and consent of a broadly representative international body such as the United Nations.

Unanimously adopted at the annual meeting in Prague on 30 September 2010. Replaced by the 2026 Update.

April 1989Declaration of Principles Concerning Activities Following the Detection of Extraterrestrial IntelligenceSuperseded

The original post-detection protocols. The discoverer should verify that extraterrestrial intelligence is the most plausible explanation before any public announcement, alert the other parties to the Declaration and national authorities, then notify the IAU Central Bureau for Astronomical Telegrams, the UN Secretary-General (under Article XI of the Outer Space Treaty) and a list of international scientific bodies. Confirmed detections are to be disseminated openly, with data shared and permanently archived, and the relevant radio frequencies protected through the ITU. Principle 8 states that no reply should be sent until appropriate international consultations have taken place. Principle 9 commits the IAA SETI Committee to continuing review and to setting up an international post-detection committee.

Approved in April 1989 by the IAA Board of Trustees and the Board of Directors of the International Institute of Space Law. Endorsed over the following three years by COSPAR, the IAU, URSI Commission J and the IAF. Replaced by the 2010 Declaration. This copy was printed from the IAA SETI Committee website (iaaseti.org) in September 2026.

Assessment scales 2

Two scales developed within the Committee to put a number on events that are otherwise hard to judge. The Rio Scale rates the significance of a claimed detection of extraterrestrial intelligence; the San Marino Scale rates the potential impact of a deliberate transmission from Earth.

2005 (adopted 2007)The San Marino ScaleAdopted by the Committee

The counterpart to the Rio Scale for transmissions from Earth. It rates the potential exposure of a deliberate transmission or reply on a scale of 1 to 10, as SMI = I + C, where I measures signal intensity relative to the Sun's background at the same frequency and C reflects the information content. It gives the debate over messaging to extraterrestrial intelligence (METI) a common measure.

Proposed in San Marino in March 2005 at the 6th World Symposium on the Exploration of Space and Life in the Universe. Endorsed and adopted by the Committee in Hyderabad in September 2007. This copy was printed from iaaseti.org in September 2026.

2000 (adopted 2002)The Rio ScaleAdopted by the Committee

An ordinal scale from 0 to 10 for rating the significance of any public claim of evidence for extraterrestrial intelligence, modelled on the Torino asteroid-impact scale. The value is RS = Q × d, where Q combines the class of phenomenon, the type of discovery and the distance, and d is the assessed credibility. It is meant to be revised as a claim is investigated. The 2010 Declaration recommends it for explaining candidate signals to non-specialists.

Proposed at the 51st IAC in Rio de Janeiro in October 2000. Adopted by the Committee in 2002; Revision 1.2 accepted in Bremen in 2003. A revised Rio Scale 2.0 was presented to the Committee in 2017. This copy was printed from iaaseti.org in September 2026.

October 2023Revising the SETI Post-Detection Protocols for the 2020s and Beyond: A Report on Work in ProgressPublished paperPost-detection

The first public report from the task group set up in 2022 to revise the 2010 Declaration. It traces the protocols from 1989 and explains why revision is needed: social media and repeated false alarms have changed how information moves, and technosignature searches at Breakthrough Listen, the SETI Institute, FAST and elsewhere have grown in scale and scope, drawing in researchers who may not see themselves as working on SETI.

Presented at the 74th International Astronautical Congress, Baku, 2–6 October 2023. Copyright the authors; released to the IAF to publish.

About

This site

Maintained by the IAA SETI Committee to keep its meeting records and the texts of the post-detection protocols together in one place that anyone can read.

The summaries were written for this site. The documents themselves are the authoritative source.

Copyright

The Committee's meeting records, presentations and reports are placed in the public domain with the agreement of their authors. Texts issued by the International Academy of Astronautics are reproduced for reference, and copyright in them stays with the IAA. Papers whose copyright is held by the International Astronautical Federation are reproduced with the permission of the IAF and their authors. Obituaries published elsewhere are linked rather than copied.

Sources

Some copies were printed from the Committee's earlier website, iaaseti.org, and carry the print date. Where an official copy exists elsewhere, the entry links to it.

The current Declaration is published by the IAA at iaaspace.org.
